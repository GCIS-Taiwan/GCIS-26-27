Junior Science Detectives: Animal Classification & Habitats

Central Idea: Humans interact with the environment which affects all the living things within the environment.
TL;DR Unit Summary
  • Building Foundations First: We begin by teaching students how the animal kingdom is organised (vertebrates vs. invertebrates, and animal classes) before using these concepts to solve animal mysteries.
  • The Narrative: Students act as "Junior Science Detectives," gathering physical evidence (skin, teeth, offspring traits, rocks, shadow movement) to identify mystery specimens and their habitats.
  • Curriculum Integration: Combines Cambridge Stage 2 Science, English Language non-fiction sorting and writing, and Mathematics (counting patterns, arrays, complements of 20).
  • How You Can Help: Ask your child: "Which animal class did you explore today, and what makes it special?"

Unit Narrative & Inquiry Approach

Welcome to Unit 1! To build strong scientific thinking, our Grade 2 learners start by developing a foundational understanding of animal taxonomy. Once students understand how scientists organise animals into groups based on internal and external features, they use this knowledge as Junior Science Detectives solving mystery case files.

Each week, detective teams receive fresh physical evidence—such as close-up photos of body coverings, dental prints, growth files, rock samples, and solar shadow logs. Students analyze the clues, test their ideas against what they know about animal classes, and determine where each animal lives and how human actions affect its habitat.

8-Week Investigation Roadmap

Week 1
The Backbone Code
Discovering the big division in the Animal Kingdom: Vertebrates vs. Invertebrates.
Week 2
Classifying the Suspects
Sorting fiction/non-fiction books into the 6 major vertebrate classes (Mammals, Birds, Reptiles, Amphibians, Fish, Invertebrates) and sketching animal profiles.
Week 3
Case Clue 1: Skin & Armor
Inspecting external body coverings (fur, feathers, scales, damp skin,) using thinking routines and comparing them to human skin.
Week 4
Case Clue 2: Bite Marks & Diets
Comparing human teeth types (incisors, molars) to animal jaws; connecting diet to health, hygiene, and nutrition.
Week 5
Case Clue 3: Family Traits & Growth
Matching parents to offspring; exploring inherited features, developmental changes over time, and signs of health/illness.
Week 6
Case Clue 4: Habitats & Shadows
Mapping hot/cold and wet/dry habitats; tracking shadow lengths to understand the apparent movement of the Sun and animal daily rhythms.
Week 7
Case Clue 5: Ground Evidence & Human Impact
Testing rock properties (quarries, mines, riverbeds) and examining how human activities alter local and global habitats.
Week 8
Case Closed: Final Dossier
Synthesizing evidence files, writing non-fiction reports, revealing mystery animals, and committing to positive environmental action.

Integrated Learning Goals

Science (Cambridge Primary Stage 2)

  • Classification & Structure: Identify animal classes (mammals, reptiles, birds, amphibians, fish, invertebrates); compare external coverings and human vs. animal teeth.
  • Life Processes: Recognize health requirements (diet, hygiene, movement), signs of illness, and inherited traits from parents to offspring.
  • Ecosystems: Define natural habitats and evaluate variations in climate, plant density, and animal populations.
  • Planet Earth & Space: Compare rock types and extraction methods; identify human impacts on the environment; observe the Sun's daily movement.

English Language Skills

  • Reading & Comprehension: Sort texts by non-fiction features, decode unfamiliar scientific words using phonics, split compound words (e.g., back-bone), and answer key question words.
  • Writing & Research: Take notes on animal classes, build vocabulary collections, write simple descriptions, and publish a final non-fiction report.

Mathematics

  • Counting & Sequences: Count and estimate up to 100, identify odd and even numbers, and skip-count in 2s, 5s, and 10s.
  • Operations & Concepts: Solve addition/subtraction problems (complements of 20), model teeth patterns with arrays/repeated addition, and understand division as equal sharing.

Supporting Your Junior Detective at Home

  • Classify Together: Point out animals in daily life or books and ask: "Is it a mammal, bird, reptile, amphibian, fish, or invertebrate? How do you know?"
  • Observe Skin & Coverings: Notice fur on pets, feathers on birds, or smooth skin on frogs during outdoor walks.
  • Read Non-Fiction Books: Visit the library to look through non-fiction books about habitats, rocks, and animal survival.
